● First, your two questions.

  Why the PDFs sit in a local cache and not the database. A PDF is a raw binary
  the code reads once. What the chatbot needs is the text pulled out of it,
  split into small passages with page numbers so every answer can cite its
  source. That text is what belongs in the database. I'll add these two things
  now:
  - Transcript and annual-report text, plus its passages, stored in the DB. The
    chatbot searches and cites from there.
  - Filing text stored in the DB too, so the chatbot can quote the company's
    filings.

  The PDF files stay on disk only as a "don't download twice" cache. Cloud hosts
  like Render wipe the disk on every deploy, so anything the app needs must
  live in the DB, and that's now the rule.

  Why it was slow. Three reasons:
  1. Every request ran one at a time, with a 1-second pause per website.
  2. Every filing's PDF was downloaded, even routine ones.

  The fix is to run different websites in parallel, download only the PDFs a
  card actually needs, and fetch prices concurrently. I'll measure the time
